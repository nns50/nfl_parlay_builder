### Two scan bugs found and fixed this run
1. **Spread pairing.** The scan keyed two-sided rungs on `(market, point)`. For spreads that
   is wrong: the complement of `GB −1.5` is `MIN +1.5`, not `MIN −1.5` (two books disagreeing
   on who is favoured both post a `−1.5`, and pairing them devigs one side of the market
   against itself). Corrected pairing — `(teamX, +p)` against `(teamY, −p)` — takes the board
   from **59 rungs to 86**, i.e. the previous scans were blind to ~30% of the priced market.
   The verdict does not change (0 arbs before, 0 after), but the coverage claim in runs 1-5
   was overstated and the spread rungs they did report were mispaired.
2. **Push mass on the wrong side.** The first key-number pass used `P(|margin| = 3) = 14.65%`
   as the push mass for a `+3` dog. Wrong: the dog winning by 3 is a **win**, not a push. The
   correct quantity is `P(favourite wins by exactly 3)`, and conditioned on the line it is
   **9.25%** (112/1211, spreads 2.0-4.0, REG 2015-25) — a 5.4pp overstatement that manufactured
   five phantom "+2 to +5pp" edges.

**Doctrinal finding worth keeping:** devigging a *pushable* pair yields P(win | no push), and the
break-even for a pushable bet is exactly `1/(1+b)` in those same terms — the push mass cancels.
So whenever the market posts a two-sided price **at** the key number, `key_number_edge` is
structurally incapable of firing; it reduces to the ordinary vig test. It can only bite as a
half-point-buy question across rungs *within one book* — which this board prices efficiently.
That is the mechanism behind run 4's "27/27 ruled out", which had the right answer without the reason.
